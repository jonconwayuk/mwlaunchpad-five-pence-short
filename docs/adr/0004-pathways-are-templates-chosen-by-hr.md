# Pathways are templates that HR chooses and applies; nothing is matched automatically

A Journey is a discrete, self-contained set of instructions that never refers to another Journey. All logic between Journeys lives in the Pathway, on Pathway Items: Dependencies (which Pathway Items, Containers or Timers must be met first) and whether each Journey is mandatory. Keeping Journeys free of dependencies lets one Journey be built once and reused in any Pathway, by owners who do not know each other's Journeys.

Dependencies form a graph, so one rule gives both sequence and parallel work: a Pathway Item with no unmet Dependencies is available. We chose this over an ordered list because real onboarding has many things happening at once, and a list cannot show that.

A Pathway is a template that an HR Partner builds and publishes for a kind of starter, such as "Branch colleague" or "Software engineer". Journeys and Pathways are tagged with job family, role, grade, platform and location from fixed lists, but only so people can browse and filter the libraries. Nothing selects Journeys or Pathways for a Person automatically. When a new starter is selected, HR filters the Pathway library and applies the right Pathway. After that, HR or the starter's Line Manager can add Journeys to that starter's Applied Pathway (Additions), but no one can change or remove the Pathway's own items. If a starter needs something different, HR builds and publishes a different Pathway.

We rejected automatic matching, where rules on Pathway Items include or exclude Journeys by Person attributes. It hides decisions inside rules that HR and managers cannot easily see or check, and it makes one Pathway harder to understand as it grows. Scale comes instead from reuse: Journeys are built once by the teams that own them and reused across many Pathways, Pathways are reused across many starters, and Additions cover what is specific to one starter or team.

The cost is more Pathways in the library, one per kind of starter, and HR must choose the right one. Library filters keep that quick, and observability flags Additions that keep being made so the Pathway owner can publish them in the live version.

A Pathway cannot reroute a starter to a different Journey part-way through. That is a production-path item. The one way a Pathway reacts to events mid-journey is to *stop* work. A Hard Stop fires when a Pathway Item ends with a Failed Outcome (such as failed vetting), or when a Timer deadline passes and the Pathway Item it watches is not Done. A Hard Stop never acts on its own: a person must Confirm, Extend (deadlines only) or Dismiss it, with a recorded reason. We allow this because a bank cannot keep progressing a starter who has failed vetting, and we keep a person in the loop because withdrawing an offer automatically would be indefensible.
